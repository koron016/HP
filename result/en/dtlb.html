Interview prep

Answering “what's your weakness?”

  • I over-researched and decided too late. Now I fix the decision date before I start looking.
  • I leaned on others before thinking it through. Now I bring my own draft first, then ask.
  • I peak under pressure, so I started too late. Now I set my own interim deadline.
  • I had views but was slow to voice them. Now I make myself speak at least once in every meeting.

What they will press you on

  • “How do you decide when the information isn't there?” → Pair a time hesitation cost you something with what you changed after
  • “What have you achieved on your own?” → Inside the group story, cut out the part that was specifically yours
  • “Are you bad at planning ahead?” → Own the late start, then explain the method that gets you there anyway
  • “Tell me about a time you took the initiative.” → From the support story, extract one moment that was your own call

What to make the centre of your story

  • The reasoning behind your choices — few applicants can actually write this
  • How many people you moved, and how — numbers land here
  • The last-minute recovery — crisis plus rescue is a strong pair
  • How the person you supported changed — write it through their change

A weakness lands when you pair it with what you now do about it. Rewrite these in your own words before using them.

How far they will dig

Deciding — Data

  1. How do you decide without enough information?
  2. Has hesitation ever stopped you deciding?
  3. What did that cost you?

How to hold your ground

“Nothing really” is the worst answer here. Naming what it cost you shows honesty and that you learned, in one move.

Working — Together

  1. What have you achieved alone?
  2. How far did you get without help?
  3. Why didn't you bring anyone in there?

How to hold your ground

“There was nobody to ask” sounds weak. “I judged that this part was mine to carry” turns the same fact into initiative.

Timing — Late

  1. Are you bad at working to a plan?
  2. Why do you think you start late?
  3. Has it ever made you miss a deadline?

How to hold your ground

Saying “never” at the third step sounds false. Admit it happened once, then name the mechanism you built afterwards. Admitting it is what buys trust.

Standing — Backbone

  1. Tell me about taking initiative
  2. Did someone ask you to start?
  3. Why didn't you move before being asked?

How to hold your ground

“I was watching how it went” sounds passive. Say you were waiting for the conditions, or checking the ground first — frame the waiting as a decision.

Interviewers dig three levels into the same story. Candidates come apart at the second and third. Prepare these and you won't.

How to size up a company

Conditions that suit you

  • Decisions actually run on numbers, not vibes
  • Team-first by default, with people close enough to ask
  • How you get there is up to you; you're judged on the result
  • Defined roles, so expertise actually compounds

Ask them this

「How do you tell whether something worked?」

How to read their answer

○They quote a specific metric they watch weekly

△They laugh and say “gut feel” — evidence is not the currency here

×They cannot name a single number — your strength will go unread

「How many people usually work on one thing?」

How to read their answer

○They give a real number: “two or three of us on anything”

△“Mostly solo, but you can ask” — probably solo in practice

×“Everyone sits apart and works their own way” — little room to pull people in

「Do people choose their own approach, or follow a set process?」

How to read their answer

○“How you do it is yours. We look at the result.”

△“There is a daily report, technically” — always check how heavy

×“You follow the documented process” — no room for your late surge

「What role would I actually start in?」

How to read their answer

○They name the role and its scope without hesitating

△“You’ll get to try lots of things” — unclear what you would become

×“A bit of everything” — expertise will not compound

Warning signs

  • “We just try things.” Nobody can quote a number.
  • Everyone works solo. Nobody can see what anyone else is doing.
  • Daily progress reporting. The process is prescribed in detail.
  • “You'll do a bit of everything.” The role can't be described.

These double as your questions for them. Judge the fit yourself from the answers — a bad fit is not a bad company.
